EchoCam

AI Baby Monitor Product Strategy

A product strategy case study for an AI-powered baby monitor designed to support disabled families through thoughtful hardware and software integration.

RoleProduct Lead

TimelineApr 2025 - Dec 2025

Market12K+ target users

FocusRoadmap, feature prioritization, feasibility

Problem

Balancing care, accessibility, and technical feasibility.

EchoCam explored how an AI-powered baby monitor could better serve disabled families. The product needed to be useful, technically realistic, financially viable, and sensitive to everyday caregiving needs.

Opportunity

Design a care product around confidence, not just monitoring.

Families needed support that could reduce uncertainty without adding another complicated device to manage. The opportunity was to connect alerts, visibility, and accessibility into a product strategy that felt practical for real caregiving contexts.

Ownership

I led the roadmap thinking across product, market, and feasibility.

I translated research into feature priorities, coordinated hardware and software considerations, and shaped the business case around target users, unit cost, and long-term product viability.

Design Strategy

Prioritize the features that make caregiving decisions easier.

The strategy centered on high-signal alerts, accessible interaction patterns, and a realistic hardware and software roadmap so the concept could support families while staying technically grounded.

01

Research

Conducted competitive analysis and identified product opportunities where existing baby monitors fell short for accessibility-focused family use cases.

02

Prioritize

Defined the roadmap by weighing user needs, market opportunity, technical feasibility, and business constraints across hardware and software decisions.

03

Coordinate

Coordinated 10+ hardware and software components toward a projected $200 unit cost while shaping the product strategy around a $7.5M projected valuation.

Outcome

A roadmap for a technically grounded care product.

The final strategy forecasted 12K+ target users and connected market sizing, feature prioritization, and product feasibility into a clearer plan for an AI baby monitor designed around disabled families.
